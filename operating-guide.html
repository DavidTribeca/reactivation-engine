How a call actually happens

Worth knowing, because it's simpler than it sounds and it's the same path you use by hand today.

1. At 4pm the engine picks who's due.
2. It writes the tag ai call on each of them in Follow Up Boss — exactly what your "Send To Simpletalk" automation does.
3. The tag makes SimpleTalk dial.
4. The outcome comes back two ways: a GoHighLevel webhook, plus a nightly cross-check against your ISA queue so a missed webhook can't cause a repeat call.
5. Anyone reached gets stop ai call and never hears from it again.

The three commands you'd ever type

Railway → reactivation-engine → Console tab. That's a terminal inside the running service.

node scripts/preflight.js            # is everything ready, and if not, why
node scripts/run-dispatch.js status  # the funnel, by wave
node scripts/run-dispatch.js stop "reason"   # HALT EVERY CALL

The stop command takes effect on the very next run. No deploy, no waiting on me. The reason and your name get recorded. Resume with resume. And the stop ai call tag in Follow Up Boss still pulls one individual person out, exactly as it does today.

Watching it, once it's running

Daily, ten seconds. The dashboard. One word at the top: RUNNING, THROTTLED, BLOCKED or STOPPED.

Weekly, ten minutes. Look at the last seven days on that page. Green means it steps volume up 25% by itself. Yellow means it's holding. Red means it already halved volume and the reason column says which of the three signals tripped. That ten minutes is the entire operating cost of this thing.

Monthly, one number. Estimated months remaining, recomputed from your actual throughput rather than my projection. If it starts climbing, something upstream changed.

Any time something breaks: Slack, #ai-calling-alerts. Every safety mechanism here fails closed, so the program can stop itself safely and completely silently — that channel is how a silent halt stops looking like a quiet day.
