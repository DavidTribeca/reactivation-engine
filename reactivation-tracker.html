How the system meters itself

Three independent ceilings govern how much is in flight at any moment. Every dispatcher run takes the smallest of them, so no single mechanism has to be right on its own.

1 · Daily target

Set by the throttle from the trailing 7 days. Green steps volume up 25%, yellow holds, red halves it. Floor 100, ceiling 750.

2 · Window share

Spreads the day across dialing windows — 45% by mid-morning, 75% cumulative by late afternoon, 100% by evening. Stops the 10am run dumping a whole day at once.

3 · In-flight ceiling

Never more than 60% of the daily target awaiting an outcome. If SimpleTalk slows, outcomes stop arriving, in-flight stays high, and intake pauses on its own.

Adding is automatic; removing is event-driven. A contact enters when next_eligible_at comes due and there's room under all three ceilings. It leaves in-flight the moment an outcome lands — reached, booked, opted out, or bad number are terminal; a no-answer goes back on the ladder with a new date. Anything stuck longer than 20 hours gets reaped as a no-answer, so a missed webhook can't park someone forever.

At a 750/day peak that means roughly 340 in flight at the busiest point, about 180 genuinely new people entering per day, and the remaining ~570 dials are retries on people already in the system.

Once live, these numbers come from the database

Every figure above is a projection until the engine is running. After deployment these views return the real thing:

node scripts/run-dispatch.js status     # funnel by cohort + last 7 days of throttle

SELECT * FROM re_v_flow;                -- in-flight now, due today, due tomorrow
SELECT * FROM re_v_today_by_window;     -- is the day spreading or piling up?
SELECT * FROM re_v_forward_load;        -- retries already committed to future days
SELECT * FROM re_v_burndown;            -- % complete + estimated months remaining
SELECT * FROM re_v_number_health;       -- per-number answer rate vs pool median
SELECT * FROM re_v_window_performance;  -- does the rotating ladder actually beat same-time?

re_v_burndown is the one to watch weekly — it recomputes estimated months remaining from your actual 14-day throughput, so it corrects the 6.2-month projection with reality rather than assumption.
